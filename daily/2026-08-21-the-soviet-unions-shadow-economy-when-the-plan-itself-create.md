# The Soviet Union’s Shadow Economy: When the Plan Itself Created the Black Market

> The state’s central plan didn’t just fail to eliminate the black market — it coexisted with a vast unofficial economy.

## Why this is interesting
The Soviet Union is often remembered as a command economy where the state controlled everything. Yet beneath the official five-year plans thrived a vast "second economy" [3]. The twist? The rigid formal system didn’t merely tolerate this shadow market — it operated alongside it.

## First principles
An informal economy is any economic activity that is neither taxed nor monitored by government [1]. In the Soviet Union, this took the form of a "second economy" — the black market or informal sector operating alongside the state-run economy [3]. The core mechanic is simple: when a formal system fails to deliver what people need — goods, services, or opportunities — people improvise outside it. The second economy filled gaps left by the formal system.

## A real-world case
The Soviet Union itself is the case: its "second economy" was the black market or informal sector of the economy [3]. Illustrative: imagine a factory manager whose official quota requires 10,000 bolts, but the state supply chain delivers only 6,000. To meet the target, he trades surplus steel from his own allocation with another factory for the missing bolts — an informal transaction that keeps both plants "on plan" but violates the rules. This kind of improvisation was not an exception; it was the glue that made the planned economy function.
